\begin{abstract}
We study whether locally generated and aggressively filtered synthetic data can
improve low-resource joint intent classification and slot filling in Traditional
Chinese as used in Taiwan.  Starting from a 20-shot-per-intent subset of MASSIVE
\texttt{zh-TW}, a local open-weight teacher generates four kinds of examples and
a fixed seven-stage pipeline filters them.  The public corpus contains 3,754
released rows.  Under a frozen 500-step QLoRA contract, adding filtered synthetic
data to the real-only baseline improves three-seed Gemma intent accuracy and
exact match by +4.14 and +3.86 percentage points, respectively.  A preregistered
replication with Phi-4-mini yields corresponding gains of +5.09 and +4.71
percentage points.  A single-seed equal-size recipe ablation finds that no
leave-one-recipe-out exact-match difference reaches the preregistered 2.5-point
detectability threshold.  We release the corpus, one Gemma adapter, code,
machine-readable reports, checksums, and resource accounting.  This document is
an evidence-bounded technical report rather than a peer-reviewed publication.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Natural language understanding (NLU) systems for intent classification and slot
filling require examples that cover both label semantics and local linguistic
variation.  This requirement is difficult in a low-resource locale such as
Traditional Chinese as used in Taiwan (\texttt{zh-TW}).  MASSIVE provides a
parallel multilingual benchmark with intent and slot annotations
\citep{fitzgerald2023massive}, but a deployment team may still have only a small
labeled subset available for adaptation.

FormosaNLU-Synth asks a deliberately narrow question: under one frozen low-
resource MASSIVE \texttt{zh-TW} contract, can synthetic examples produced by a
local open-weight teacher improve a smaller student over the same real-only
baseline?  The project separates generation, filtering, training, and evaluation;
keeps the test split outside every construction decision; and preserves negative
results rather than adjusting thresholds after inspection.

The evidence supports three bounded conclusions.  First, filtered synthetic data
improves the preregistered paired comparison for two student model families.
Second, the result survives deterministic robustness probes, although uncertainty
is substantial with three seeds.  Third, a single-seed equal-size composition
study does not isolate a detectable independent contribution from any one recipe.
These findings do not establish generalization to other datasets, languages,
model families, or training budgets.

\section{Related Work}
MASSIVE contains one million virtual-assistant utterances across 51 languages,
18 domains, 60 intents, and 55 slot types \citep{fitzgerald2023massive}.  The
present work uses its Traditional Chinese (Taiwan) partition and retains its
joint intent and slot structure.

Synthetic instruction-data pipelines can bootstrap supervision from model
generations and then reject invalid or overly similar examples.  Self-Instruct
demonstrated this generate--filter--fine-tune pattern for instruction following
\citep{wang2023selfinstruct}.  FormosaNLU-Synth applies the general pattern to a
fixed structured-prediction schema rather than claiming a new general-purpose
instruction-generation algorithm.

The student models are adapted with low-rank updates.  LoRA freezes pretrained
weights and learns low-rank matrices \citep{hu2022lora}; QLoRA extends this idea
to backpropagation through quantized base weights \citep{dettmers2023qlora}.
The primary student is the Apache-2.0 Gemma 4 E4B instruction model
\citep{gemma4modelcard}.  The replication student is the MIT-licensed
Phi-4-mini instruction model \citep{phi4minimodelcard}.

\section{Method}
\paragraph{Real-data budget.}
For each MASSIVE intent, the low-resource training set contains at most 20 real
examples selected with seed 42, producing 1,176 real examples.  Validation and
test rows never enter generation or training-data assembly.

\paragraph{Local generation.}
The teacher is \texttt{qwen3.6:27b}, served locally through Ollama.  It produces
four recipe families: label-preserving paraphrases, slot substitution followed
by constrained rewriting, locale-aware noise and code-switching, and hard
negatives between confusable intents.  Labels are constructed by the pipeline;
there is no row-by-row human annotation.  The teacher, primary student, and
independent judge belong to different model families.

\paragraph{Fixed filtering.}
The pipeline applies schema validation (F1), label-contract checks (F2), grounded
slot checks (F3), Taiwan locale and language checks (F4), seed-copy, duplicate,
and outlier removal (F5), and validation/test contamination exclusion (F6).
These fixed stages reduce 11,264 generated rows to 3,760.  A stratified F7 audit
with the independent \texttt{gpt-oss:20b} judge rejects six rows from the public
release, leaving 3,754 rows.  The already frozen training experiments retain the
3,760-row F1--F6 corpus; F7 is a release-only exclusion and does not retroactively
change the experimental contract.

The largest removal is synthetic near-duplication: 4,596 rows are rejected as
near duplicates.  The preregistered target of 8,000--9,000 accepted examples is
therefore missed.  Thresholds were not relaxed after this shortfall was observed.

\paragraph{Structured output.}
Each model must emit strict JSON containing one legal intent and a list of typed
slot spans.  Metrics are intent accuracy, intent macro-F1, slot micro-F1, exact
match, and strict JSON-valid rate.  Exact match requires the complete structured
prediction to satisfy the evaluator.

\section{Experimental Design}
The primary matrix uses Gemma 4 E4B with 4-bit QLoRA.  Every trained condition
uses 500 optimizer steps and the same prompt template without a label catalog.
The deployment-oriented zero-shot baseline uses a separate prompt containing the
legal label catalog; it is not a same-prompt causal ablation.  All headline
evaluations use the untouched 2,974-row MASSIVE \texttt{zh-TW} test set.

The seed-42 matrix compares real-only training, conventional augmentation,
unfiltered synthetic data at full and equal size, the filtered corpus, and the
full-real upper reference.  The primary paired analysis then trains real-only and
real-plus-filtered conditions with seeds 42, 43, and 44 under identical data and
configuration hashes.  A 5,000-repetition hierarchical paired bootstrap first
resamples seeds and then paired test rows within each selected seed.  Exact
McNemar tests are computed per seed for intent accuracy and exact match, with
Holm correction over six tests.

Before observing the second-family results, the cross-family criterion was fixed:
the paired mean delta for intent accuracy and exact match had to be positive in
both families, and every corresponding hierarchical 95\% confidence-interval
lower bound had to exceed zero.  Results are summarized per family and are not
pooled.

Across the three paired Gemma seeds, filtered synthetic data improves intent
accuracy by \(4.14 \pm 1.39\) percentage points and exact match by
\(3.86 \pm 0.73\) points (mean \(\pm\) sample standard deviation).  The
hierarchical bootstrap estimates are +4.14 with 95\% CI [2.60, 5.59] for intent
accuracy and +3.86 with CI [2.75, 4.92] for exact match.  The six exact McNemar
tests for these two metrics reject after Holm correction, with adjusted
\(p \leq 0.00017\).  These tests apply only to the frozen rows and contract.

\section{Robustness}
The evaluation-only robustness manifest deterministically perturbs the test set
with colloquial/lexical variants and ASR-like noise, yielding 8,922 rows per
adapter.  It never re-enters training.  Across seeds 42--44, all five paired mean
deltas are positive for both families.  For Gemma, the filtered-minus-real-only
deltas are 3.63 intent-accuracy points, 2.11 macro-F1 points, 2.75 slot-F1 points,
3.58 exact-match points, and 1.49 JSON-valid points.  For Phi-4-mini, the
corresponding deltas are 6.22, 4.69, 3.73, 6.98, and 1.83 points.

The uncertainty is important.  Gemma macro-F1, slot F1, and JSON-valid sample
standard deviations are comparable to or larger than their means at \(n=3\).
Two Gemma metrics that are negative at seed 42 become positive when all three
seeds are included.  The perturbations are deterministic probes, not a measured
distribution of real speech-recognition errors; they support a bounded stress
test rather than a general robustness claim.

\section{Cross-Family Replication}
Phi-4-mini repeats the real-only versus real-plus-filtered comparison using the
same corpus, prompt template, 500 steps, seeds, evaluator, and 2,974 test rows.
The Phi intent-accuracy mean delta is +5.09 percentage points with hierarchical
95\% CI [1.83, 9.02], and the exact-match delta is +4.71 with CI [1.36, 7.59].
The corresponding Gemma estimates are +4.14 [2.60, 5.59] and +3.86 [2.75, 4.92].
Both preregistered primary metrics therefore pass the cross-family criterion.

This result is not uniformly positive for every metric and test.  Gemma's
JSON-valid interval crosses zero, and one Phi seed-level exact-match McNemar test
does not reject after correction.  The conclusion is consequently limited to
replication across two families under this one frozen dataset and training
contract; it is not a claim about arbitrary models.

\section{Equal-N Recipe Ablation}
The M19 composition experiment fixes each condition at 1,176 real plus 2,246
synthetic rows.  An equal-size control retains all four recipes; four other
conditions each exclude one recipe.  Every condition uses Gemma, seed 42, 500
steps, and the same 2,974-row test set.  Exact-match difference from control and
a threshold of 2.5 percentage points were fixed before execution.

The four exact-match deltas were +0.50 +2.02 -0.74 and +1.31 percentage points;
none reaches the threshold.  This is a descriptive comparison with a single seed
(\(n=1\)).  It neither shows that the recipes are useless nor identifies an
independent recipe effect.  In particular, this result does not support a recipe-level causal claim.
No additional seed, new metric, or lower threshold
was added after inspecting the result.

\section{Limitations}
The core evidence uses one translated benchmark locale, two student families,
three training seeds, a fixed 500-step budget, and one teacher/judge setup.
MASSIVE \texttt{zh-TW} is translated from English SLURP and may not cover the
distribution of naturally occurring Taiwanese speech.  Synthetic examples can
inherit teacher biases, local-knowledge gaps, and generation modes.  The F7
random stratum contains only 50 rows; its observed 6.0\% rejection rate has a
wide Wilson interval and targeted strata are not an unbiased corpus estimate.

M19 is a single seed and is intentionally underpowered for small recipe effects.
Per-intent estimates vary considerably across seeds, so extreme seed-42 changes
must not be interpreted as stable intent-specific effects.  Robustness probes
are generated perturbations rather than sampled production errors.  There is no
end-user study, no wall-socket energy measurement, and no evidence for other
languages or domains.  The report is not peer reviewed.

\section{Resource Accounting}
Every model workload ran locally on one NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 24 GB, with no
paid model API usage.  The frozen primary path accounts for 14.440 GPU hours.
Including generation audits, extra seeds, robustness, Phi replication, and M19,
the traceable local total is 42.412 hours.

At the documented 450 W GPU TDP, the total corresponds to a 19.085 kWh
GPU-only upper-bound envelope.  This value is not a wall-socket measurement and
does not imply that the GPU operated at TDP throughout.  One interrupted M19
attempt contributes 2.084 hours through a hash-anchored runtime audit so that
discarded compute is not silently omitted.

\texttt{f70f423814dcd47943c92c0beb8b08a4e7f65e60a44355d3dcd95bed9f0bd60a}.
Machine-readable reports record row counts, data and prediction hashes, fixed
seeds, model revisions, bootstrap settings, and interpretation boundaries.

Repository checks rebuild reported README values from tracked reports, audit the
sole-contributor history, verify documented commands, and validate publication
metadata.  The source reports are authoritative if prose and machine-readable
evidence differ.  Reproduction should use the tagged environment and documented
commands rather than rerunning generation with newer upstream model revisions.
